# Educational AI FAQ
## Common Questions and Concerns About AI in Education

---

## ⚖️ Academic Integrity and Authenticity

### Q: Should we teach students about appropriate AI use?
**A:** Absolutely. Digital literacy now includes AI literacy:
- **Transparent policies** about when and how AI tools should be used
- **Explicit instruction** on ethical AI collaboration
- **Assessment methods** that value the learning process alongside final products
- **Regular discussions** about AI's role in professional development

---

## 🎯 Educational Effectiveness

### Q: Do students actually learn better with AI assistance?
**A:** Research indicates several learning benefits:
- **Immediate feedback** increases retention and correction speed
- **Personalised pacing** accommodates different learning styles
- **Safe practice environments** build confidence before high-stakes situations
- **Increased engagement** through interactive dialogue
- However, effectiveness depends on **thoughtful implementation** and **appropriate use cases**

### Q: Will students become dependent on AI assistance?
**A:** Proper implementation builds independence:
- **Scaffolding approach** gradually reduces AI support
- **Metacognitive development** helps students understand their own learning processes
- **Critical thinking emphasis** ensures students evaluate AI suggestions
- **Regular assessment** without AI assistance maintains independent skill development

### Q: How do we measure the impact of AI tools on learning outcomes?
**A:** Establish baseline measurements before implementation:
- **Pre/post assessments** of specific skills
- **Engagement metrics** including time-on-task and interaction quality
- **Student self-efficacy surveys** measuring confidence and motivation
- **Long-term retention testing** to verify sustained learning
- **Qualitative feedback** from both students and instructors

## 👥 Faculty and Staff Concerns

### Q: Will AI replace teachers?
**A:** No. AI enhances rather than replaces human educators:
- **AI handles** routine tasks like initial feedback and skill practice
- **Educators focus** on higher-level thinking, creativity, and emotional support
- **Human judgment** remains essential for complex learning situations
- **Relationship building** and mentorship cannot be automated
- **Professional growth** often increases as educators master new tools

## 📊 Assessment and Evaluation

### Q: How do we assess student work created with AI assistance?
**A:** Evolving assessment strategies:
- **Process-focused evaluation** examining learning journey documentation
- **Skill demonstration** in live or supervised environments
- **Reflection requirements** on AI collaboration and learning
- **Portfolio approaches** showing growth over time
- **Authentic assessment** using real-world application scenarios

### Q: Should we change our grading criteria?
**A:** Consider gradual adaptations:
- **Learning process** weighted alongside final products
- **Critical thinking** about AI suggestions and alternatives
- **Collaboration skills** including effective AI interaction
- **Metacognitive reflection** on learning strategies
- **Transparency requirements** about AI assistance received

**Q: Is AI going to replace university faculty?**

A: No. The evidence from six months of experimentation shows AI changes faculty work rather than eliminates it. Faculty spend less time on data compilation and more on interpretation, mentoring, and pedagogical design. The work becomes more valuable, not obsolete.

**Q: How much does AI integration actually cost?**

A: It varies widely. Some approaches (using freely available tools like ChatGPT or Claude) have minimal cost. Others (custom tools like FLX Curriculum Curator) require institutional investment. The good news: you can start small with pilots, prove value, then scale. Most faculty experiments described in this presentation used free or existing institutional tools.

### **For Faculty & Educators**

**Q: How do I start experimenting with AI in my teaching?**

A: Start small:

1. Pick one repetitive task (e.g., generating discussion questions, initial draft of feedback)
2. Use AI for first draft, apply your expertise to refine
3. Observe: What worked? What didn't? What surprised you?
4. Share with colleagues (both successes and failures)
5. Iterate

The faculty examples in this presentation all started with small experiments, not grand transformations.

**Q: What if my students use AI to cheat?**

A: Reframe the question: "How do I design assessment where AI use reveals student thinking rather than hides it?"

Examples from our faculty:

- **Tony's approach**: Give AI-generated questions with errors; assess students' ability to find them
- **Renée's approach**: Use AI to enable more ambitious work (graphic novels); assess conceptual understanding
- **Michael's approach**: Mark AI conversations; evaluate question quality and critical evaluation

The Assessment 2030 initiative is developing frameworks for this. See [Assessment Resources](#assessment).

**Q: What's the "5-step framework" mentioned in the presentation?**

A: Framework for teaching students to critique AI responses:

1. **Question the Source** - What's the AI trained on? Known limitations?
2. **Verify Claims** - Cross-check facts, especially statistics and citations
3. **Evaluate Logic** - Does the reasoning actually hold up?
4. **Identify Bias** - What perspectives are missing?
5. **Iterate & Improve** - Use critique to refine your prompts

This teaches active evaluation rather than passive consumption of AI outputs.

### **About Assessment 2030**

**Q: What is Assessment 2030?**

A: A Curtin-wide initiative rethinking assessment design in the AI era. The project explores:

- How to design authentic assessment when AI can generate content
- Moving from "prevention" (stopping AI use) to "demonstration" (showing student thinking)
- The 50/50 principle: At least 50% of assessment should demonstrate individual student thinking
- New assessment formats that use AI as tool rather than threat

**Q: How does this relate to AI in the curriculum?**

A: Assessment design is where AI integration becomes concrete. You can teach with AI, but if your assessment isn't designed for the AI era, students will either avoid AI (and miss developing literacy) or use it inappropriately (bypassing learning).

Assessment 2030 provides frameworks for the middle path: intentional AI use that demonstrates competence.

### **Technical Questions**

**Q: What AI tools are Curtin faculty using?**

A: Most commonly mentioned:

- **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) - widely accessible, general purpose
- **Claude** (Anthropic) - preferred by some for nuanced writing tasks
- **Microsoft Copilot** - integrated with Office 365, available through Curtin
- **FLX Curriculum Curator** - custom tool for curriculum development
- **Discipline-specific tools** - various depending on field

**Q: Aren't AI outputs often inaccurate?**

A: Yes - research suggests up to 30% of AI-generated content can contain errors (hallucinations, bias, outdated information). This is precisely why:
1. Domain expertise is essential for quality control
2. We teach students to evaluate critically, not trust blindly
3. AI is best used for focused, bounded tasks with human review

The limitations aren't reasons to avoid AI - they're reasons to develop expertise in using it well.

## 🧠 Advanced AI Concepts in Education

### Q: What are Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) and how do they enhance learning?
**A:** Intelligent Tutoring Systems are AI-powered educational tools that provide personalised instruction and feedback:
- **Adaptive learning** - adjusts difficulty and content based on student performance
- **Real-time feedback** - offers immediate guidance without waiting for instructor review
- **Scalability** - supports one-to-one tutoring experiences for large groups
- **Data-driven insights** - tracks learning patterns to identify areas needing attention
- **24/7 availability** - allows students to practice anytime, anywhere

### Q: How does cognitive prompting work in educational settings?
**A:** Cognitive prompting uses AI to guide students through thinking processes:
- **Scaffolded questioning** - breaks down complex problems into manageable steps
- **Metacognitive development** - helps students understand their own learning strategies
- **Critical thinking enhancement** - encourages evaluation of information and reasoning
- **Personalised guidance** - adapts prompts based on individual student needs
- **Transferable skills** - teaches thinking frameworks applicable across subjects

### Q: What are the main challenges in implementing AI tools in pedagogy?
**A:** Key challenges include:
- **Technical integration** - ensuring compatibility with existing systems and infrastructure
- **Faculty training** - building confidence and skills in AI tool usage
- **Equity concerns** - addressing access disparities and digital divides
- **Assessment adaptation** - redesigning evaluations for AI-enhanced learning environments
- **Ethical considerations** - managing data privacy, bias, and appropriate AI use
- **Change management** - overcoming resistance and facilitating cultural shifts in education

### Q: How can AI tools be integrated with traditional teaching methods?
**A:** Effective integration strategies:
- **Blended approaches** - combine AI assistance with human instruction for optimal outcomes
- **Flipped classroom models** - use AI for content delivery, freeing class time for discussion
- **Supplemental support** - provide AI as additional resources rather than replacements
- **Collaborative learning** - leverage AI to facilitate peer interactions and group work
- **Continuous evaluation** - regularly assess integration effectiveness and make adjustments
