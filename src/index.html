Welcome

This workshop is designed for educators with varying levels of AI experience—from those who've never used AI tools to those already experimenting with them. Our goal is AI literacy through practical, hands-on experience, not technical expertise.

Quick Note: We recognize that some faculty are concerned about AI in education. This workshop addresses those concerns directly. We believe the uniquely human skills of empathy, mentorship, and creative problem-solving become more valuable as we integrate AI, not less.

What You'll Learn

🎓 Knowledge

Understand what AI can and can't do, its limitations and capabilities, and why it matters for your teaching.

💡 Skills

Master practical prompting techniques and learn to evaluate AI output critically using the 5-step critique framework.

🤝 Confidence

Gain hands-on experience in a supportive environment where all questions are welcome and your expertise is valued.

🏛️ Assessment

Explore how to design assessments that integrate—or resist—AI, connecting to the morning assessment design session.

Assessment & Personalization

We use two quick assessments to personalize your experience:

📋 Reflective Profile (Week Before)

A quick MS Forms survey to surface your concerns and motivations. You'll receive personalized pre-reading suggestions based on your responses.

Duration: 8-10 minutes | Optional

🎯 AI-Literacy Diagnostic (Day Of)

A 15-minute interactive assessment that measures your current AI literacy and recommends which workshop activities to focus on.

Duration: 12-15 minutes | Instant personalized results

Take Now

Neither assessment is a test you can "fail." They help us understand your starting point so we can meet you where you are.

Context: Morning Sessions

This workshop sits within a larger day that also includes:

🏆 Assessment Design Session

Morning session on how to design assessments that integrate or resist AI. Our Activity 4 connects directly to this work.

⚖️ Ethics Session

Morning session on ethical considerations in AI. We reference this work (privacy, bias, fairness) but don't teach it—that's the dedicated session.

Why this matters: We focus on how to use AI in your teaching. You'll use your assessment design and ethics knowledge alongside AI skills to make intentional choices.
